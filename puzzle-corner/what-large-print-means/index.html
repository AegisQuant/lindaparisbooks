What “large print” really means in a puzzle book

“Large print” is printed on many puzzle books, but there is no law that says how big the letters must be. Here is a simple way to know what you are getting before you buy.

Points, in plain words

Letter size is measured in points. One point is 1/72 of an inch, so a 72-point capital letter block is about an inch tall. Most everyday books are printed somewhere around 10 to 12 points.

The 18-point rule

The American Printing House for the Blind, which writes research-based guidelines for large-print documents, puts it simply: a document is not really large print unless the type is 18 points or larger (APH large print guidelines).

What to check on a large-print puzzle book

  1. Is the size stated? Look for a number, such as “20-point letters”. If the listing only says “large print” with no number, you cannot compare.
  2. Which part is large? The letters in the puzzle grid matter most, because that is where your eyes search. Word lists and page titles are often smaller.
  3. How many puzzles per page? One puzzle per page usually means bigger grids and bigger letters.
  4. Are the answers included? Answers in the back save frustration when one word just will not show itself.
  5. Look inside. Use “Look inside” on Amazon or the sample pages a publisher shares.

How we do it

The letter size on our covers is measured on the printed puzzle grid, and the number on the cover is the number in the book. Every puzzle is on its own page, and every answer is printed in the back. You can print two puzzles free and see for yourself.
